Shop with Kai · design notes · concept, not a spec

Shop for the job, not for products.

If we weren't held to the shopping list, cart and Shop tab we have now, this is what I'd build. It's for renovation experts who run hundreds of properties as a team. They know exactly what a job needs, and they shouldn't have to become online shoppers to buy it. Kai should do the shopping work. They should do the judging.

01Who we're designing for

Experts, not shoppersThey know what a rental kitchen needs better than any catalog.

What slows them down is everything around the choice: finding the product, doing the box math, checking stock, remembering what the owner said. The product should take that work away and leave the judgment to them.

Not tech-savvyPlain words, big targets, one obvious next step.

No icon-only buttons, no hidden gestures, no jargon. Status is always spelled out in words, not just a color. Mistakes can be undone instead of guarded with "are you sure?" dialogs.

A team that takes turnsScoping and shopping alternate, one person at a time.

A field agent scopes in one turn, purchasing shops in the next, and the manager reviews and orders in another. Whoever takes the turn has to pick up where the last person stopped, without redoing anything.

Hundreds of propertiesMost of what they buy, they've bought before.

The same white shaker cabinet, the same LVP and the same paint, job after job. The ideal is a list that arrives mostly filled in from team standards, so shopping means handling the exceptions.

02What gets in the way today

  • You can't see the shelf. A product type shows one row of four products, with arrows to see the rest. Comparing 12 floors means paging and remembering.
  • The same product is chosen over and over. LVP for four rooms is four separate choices, and they can quietly drift apart. In the demo data, Bedroom 2 has a different floor from the rest of the house.
  • The context lives somewhere else. The task description, notes and photos are on the Scope tab. Mood board and floor plan open in a side panel that squeezes the page.
  • Quantities are in the wrong units. The scope says 185 SF, but the store sells boxes of 23.6 SF. The user does that math, and gets it wrong.
  • Problems surface late. The cart is a list of items. You only find a missing pick when it's already a problem, and a substitution means a phone call.
  • An empty shelf stops the job. Any substitute product is a change order, so a crew lead at the Pro desk has to call the office and wait.
  • Hand-offs lose the thread. When the turn passes from scoping to shopping, the reasons behind a choice (the owner's note, the measurement) stay behind on the Scope tab.

03Principles

1Shop for needs, not products

The list is what the property needs. A product is an answer to a need, and a need stays a need until it's installed.

2Context travels with you

The task, quantity, photos, notes and look sit above every shelf. Media (the mood board and site photos), the floor plan, notes or a page open as one side panel next to the shelf, on the left or the right.

3See the whole room, and the whole shelf

The room view stacks every item with the room's picks side by side, for scanning and checking the look. The shelf shows one item's every product in a full grid, for deciding. No carousels either way.

4Buy units, not math

Kai turns SF into boxes and walls into gallons, using each product's own pack size. Prices show both per unit and for the whole job.

5Substitution is decided up front

The team sets once what happens when something is out of stock (replace with any similar item). Anyone can change it for one item, from the shelf or the cart, without being asked every time.

6Choose once, everywhere

The same product across rooms is one choice and one order line, with a flag when rooms drift apart.

7Check before, not after

Review & order is a readiness check: missing picks, items over their allowance, and what happens if something's out. Each one has a one-click fix.

8Built for turns

One person has the job at a time. Picks, substitutions and notes stay with the job, so whoever takes the turn next starts where the last person stopped.

9Spending is welcome

Allowances are a reference, not a cap. Kai shows the room to upgrade and never nags anyone to go cheaper.

07Change orders without stopping the job

The core idea: approve the intent, not just the SKU. A scope line approves "white 3×6 matte subway tile, about $1,040, for the kitchen backsplash", plus the product we'd like and what to do if it's out. A swap at the store that stays inside that intent isn't a change to the job, so it shouldn't need a change order.

Rule 1Chosen substitutes

"Choose a replacement now" picks up to two in advance, approved along with the product. Swapping to one is always OK: it’s a pre-approved substitution.

Rule 2Any similar item

The team's setting: same kind of product, on the mood board, within 10% or $150 of the line (the team sets the numbers). OK, logged with the reason.

Rule 3Everything else asks

The manager gets a one-tap request and the crew keeps picking up. If approved, Kai writes it into the next change order. Nobody types it twice.

The same rules cover other everyday surprises: a supplier that can't deliver in time (move the same product to another store, which is no change at all), a discontinued SKU, or a damaged box. Every swap leaves a record of who, when, why and what changed, so trusting the crew doesn't cost you visibility.

The question the crew lead asks at the Pro desk should never be "who do I call?" It should be "which of these two?"

08Working in turns

  • One person at a time: you take the turn to shop, the same way you take it to scope. "Your turn" in the bar says the job is yours. Nobody else is editing while you have it, so there's nothing live to watch.
  • Nothing is lost between turns: picks, substitutions, notes and quantities stay with the job. The next person sees what's chosen, what's left to choose, and why, from the notes on each item.
  • Scope flows into shopping: if a quantity changes in a scoping turn (Countertops 38 → 42 SF), the pick stays and the order simply buys the new amount.
  • Ordering follows the approval rule: placing orders spends money, so the job manager takes the turn to approve orders over $10,000.
  • The store doesn't need the turn: the crew lead's pickup list is a texted link. Swaps inside the rules are logged on the job. A swap outside them is a request to the manager.
  • Notes where the decision is: the notes on a shelf are the notes for that item. "Order one extra box for the hallway transition" is right there when you choose the floor.

09Across hundreds of properties

  • Team standards prefill the list. When a scope is approved, every need that has a team standard arrives already chosen and in stock near the job. In the demo, 26 of 29 needs came pre-chosen.
  • Kai learns from swaps. If a substitute keeps being used, Kai suggests making it the standard. If a store keeps running out, Kai suggests buying from another store or getting delivery.
  • Later: combine jobs. Three properties buying Shaw Paragon from the same Home Depot this week could share one pickup and one bulk price.

10The same product on more than one shelf

This is the open question we kept coming back to. The answer here is a By product view: one shelf per product type, with a row per room. You choose once for every checked room, and uncheck a room to give it its own product. If rooms drift apart, the shelf flags it with "2 different products" and offers "Use it everywhere". In Review & order, the same product from several rooms is one order line with the rooms listed. The box rounding happens once instead of per room, which is where the "Combined across rooms" line in the summary comes from. Size-sensitive products (vanities) apply only to the rooms they fit, and the shelf says which room still needs its own.

11What I'd build first

Phase 1 · fits KaiWeb todaySee the shelf, with its context

Full-grid shelves. The context band. Purchase units (boxes and gallons). The By-product view with mismatch flags. The readiness check in Review & order. None of this needs a new back end.

Phase 2Substitution and the crew

A team substitution setting, changeable per item. Swap rules in team settings. The pickup list on the phone with swaps. Swaps logged in Activity and flowing into change orders.

Phase 3The list fills itself

Team standards prefill from the template. Live store stock (Home Depot Pro and Lowe's Pro). Combining orders across jobs.

12How we'd know it works

  • Time from scope approved to all orders placed (target: same day for a standard turn).
  • Share of needs that arrive pre-chosen and get ordered unchanged.
  • Change orders caused by substitutions (target: near zero).
  • Pickups finished without a call to the office.

13Questions for you

  • A swap request from the store: does the manager have to take the turn to approve it, or can it be approved without the turn so the crew isn't kept waiting?
  • Should Shop open on the first room, on "All", or straight on the next thing left to choose? The concept opens on the Kitchen.
  • Who sets the swap rule (10% or $150): the team template, each job, or both, the way allowances work?
  • Are chosen substitutes approved explicitly (the manager sees them in the scope document), or implied by the team's substitution setting?
  • Should "Needs review" also flag rooms or departments over their allowance, or only tasks?
  • Which supplier accounts and stock feeds can we really get? That decides how live "in stock at #1863" can be.
  • Does the crew lead get a Kai login, or is the pickup list a texted link?
